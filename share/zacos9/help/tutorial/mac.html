Coming from Mac OS 9

If you used a Mac in the 1990s, you already know most of ZacOS 9. This tour takes about five minutes, in four short pages.

The keys

On a PC keyboard, ⌘ (Command) is the Windows key and Option is Alt. The shortcuts are the ones you remember: ⌘-N, ⌘-O, ⌘-W, ⌘-Q, ⌘-I for Get Info, ⌘-Delete to move to the Trash, ⌘-Y for Put Away. Control-click (or right-click) for a contextual menu.

Works the way you remember

  • Platinum windows: the close box at the left of the title bar, the zoom and collapse boxes at the right. Double-click a title bar to collapse a window into it, as WindowShade did. Option-click the close or collapse box to do the same to all of a program's windows.
  • One menu bar along the top, always showing the menus of the program in front, with the Application menu at its right end.
  • A spatial Sniffer (the Finder, by another name): each folder opens in its own window, which remembers where it was and how it looked. Icon, button and list views, labels, aliases, spring-loaded folders, pop-up windows, Get Info, Put Away.
  • Zacintosh HD at the top right of the desktop, holding Applications and the System Folder, with Control Panels, Extensions and Startup Items inside it.
  • Control panels in the Zac menu, which take effect at once. Most have the names you know: Appearance, Date & Time, General Controls, Monitors, Sound, TCP/IP, File Sharing, Startup Disk.
  • The extension parade at startup, the Extensions Manager, and Hint Bubbles in the Help menu.
